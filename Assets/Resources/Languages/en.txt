Planispheric Astrolabe
Copper, 13.6 cm in diameter, late 9th century AH.
-----------------------------------------
An astronomical instrument for observation and analog computation. A multifunctional device, it notably allows the measurement of the altitude
-----------------------------------------
of stars, including the sun, thus enabling the determination of the observation time and 
-----------------------------------------
the direction of the celestial body. Its design, of Greek origin dating back to Antiquity,
-----------------------------------------
 was much later refined by the Arabs.

Egyptian Sarcophagus
Painted and gilded wood, height: 180 cm, Ancient Egypt, New Kingdom (ca. 1550–1070 BCE).
-----------------------------------------
A central element of Egyptian funerary rites, the sarcophagus served to protect the deceased's body and ensure their rebirth in the afterlife. Often richly decorated with hieroglyphs, religious scenes, and protective symbols, it reflected the social status and beliefs of the individual. The anthropoid shape, introduced from the Middle Kingdom onward, reinforced identification with the human form.

Libyco-Berber Carved Stone
-----------------------------------------
This stone slab, inscribed in the ancient Libyco-Berber script, reflects the artistic skill and cultural expression of the Numidian era. Such inscriptions were used to convey messages, mark places, or assert identity. The script is among the oldest in North Africa, and its legacy lives on in Tifinagh, still used today by Amazigh communities.

Planispheric Astrolabe
-----------------------------------------
To ancient astronomers, the astrolabe was more than a tool — it was a window into the will of the heavens. In medieval Persian traditions, the positions of stars at one’s birth were thought to influence destiny. Scholars used instruments like this not only to measure time but to interpret omens. Some believed a misaligned star could foretell disaster.

Mummy
-----------------------------------------
In the 19th century, European collectors developed a strange obsession with Egyptian mummies. British elites, in particular, hosted "unwrapping parties" — spectacles where mummies were publicly dismantled. Even more unsettling: powdered mummy flesh, called mumia, was ground into medicine and sold across Europe. Few mummies survived this appetite for the dead.

Libyco-Berber Carved Stone
-----------------------------------------
Some carved stones bear symbols that scholars still cannot fully decipher. In ancient belief, stone held presence — memory, vigilance, perhaps even warning. These silent monuments seemed to forge a pact between the visible and the unseen. Even today, some say their inscriptions are not meant to be read after nightfall.
